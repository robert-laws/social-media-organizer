Most campuses now have an AI policy. Far fewer have AI infrastructure — and the difference is starting to show.

A policy tells people what's permitted. Infrastructure is what lets them act on it: faculty training that goes past a one-hour webinar, sandboxed tools where students can experiment without risk, staff whose actual job is answering 'is this a good use of the tool?'

There are two things to understand here. First, rules without capability produce quiet non-compliance — people use the tools anyway and stop telling you. Second, capability tends to produce better norms on its own, because judgment is learned in practice, not from a PDF.

The campuses getting this right treat AI literacy the way they treat information security: a standing function with staff and a budget, not a document reviewed annually.

Where is your institution on that spectrum?
