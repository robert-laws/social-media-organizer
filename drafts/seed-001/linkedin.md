We keep teaching patrons how to prompt. We should be teaching them how to doubt.

Tool-specific tricks have a shelf life measured in model releases. The skills that transfer are older and quieter: spotting a fabricated citation, noticing when an answer is fluent but hollow, deciding whether a task should touch a chatbot at all.

Those aren't AI skills. They're the evaluation skills librarians have always taught, applied to a source that talks back.

A workshop on 'the best ChatGPT prompts' ages out by spring. A patron who can interrogate any tool's output is set for the next decade.

How are you balancing the two in your programs?
